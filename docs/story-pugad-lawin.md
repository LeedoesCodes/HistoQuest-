# Arc 2 — Sigaw sa Pugad Lawin (The Cry of Pugad Lawin), 1896

**Status:** DRAFT for review. Not yet converted to game content.
**Audience:** Grade 5 (about 10–11 years old)
**POV:** The pupil plays a *fictional ally* — a young Katipunan messenger — never Bonifacio himself.

> ⚠️ **Read the "Historical notes" (§5) before approving.** This arc has a genuine
> scholarly controversy (the date and place of "the Cry"), plus the usual
> dramatised dialogue. Your adviser / an Araling Panlipunan teacher should sign off.

---

## 1. Design principles (same spine as Mactan)

| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Hook in the first seconds** | Open on a warning in the night — the Katipunan has been discovered. Danger first, explanation later. |
| **The child is useful, not a bystander** | The ally is a **messenger** — small and quick, unsuspected by the *guardia civil*, trusted to carry secret words. That single role motivates all three mini-games. |
| **Personal stakes before national stakes** | The people you recruit are your own neighbours and friends; the danger is to people you know. |
| **The teachable core** | A revolution began not with an army but with **ordinary people choosing courage** — spreading word in secret, and one symbolic act of refusal (tearing the cedula). |
| **Choices shape experience, not history** | Every path converges on the real outcome: the Cry, and the start of the revolution. |
| **Respect, not caricature** | The Spanish authorities are a real threat, but the focus is Filipino courage, not enemy villainy. |

**The three mini-games are woven in with real historical logic:**

1. **Code-unscramble** → the Katipunan communicated in secret codes/passwords. You decode the call to gather.
2. **Recruitment** → the Katipunan grew through secret, careful recruiting. You rally more Filipinos to come.
3. **Cedula-tear** → the climactic symbolic act at the gathering: tearing the cedula to reject Spanish rule.

*(Note: this reorders the current build, which runs cedula → recruit → code. The
new order — code → recruit → cedula — builds to the cedula-tear as the climax,
which reads far better. Conversion will reorder the nodes.)*

**Reading level:** short sentences, 2–4 lines per beat (keeps the typewriter
crisp and never overflows the panel).

## 2. Character roster

### Ikaw / You — *Batang tagapaghatid ng Katipunan (young messenger)* — **fictional**
- **Who:** An unnamed Filipino child, ~11–12, quick and clever, knows Manila's alleys.
- **Wants:** To prove that even a child can serve the cause.
- **Fears:** That one mistake of theirs could expose the whole brotherhood.
- **Superpower:** No one suspects a child. You carry secret words no one else can.

### Andres Bonifacio — *Ang Supremo ng Katipunan* — **real**
- **Who:** Leader (Supremo) of the Katipunan. From Tondo, Manila; humble beginnings, largely self-taught by reading.
- **Wants:** Kalayaan (freedom) for *every* Filipino, even the poorest.
- **Why he matters for kids:** He was not a rich general. He was an ordinary worker who believed ordinary people could be free.
- **Line (dramatised):** *"Kung hindi tayo kikilos ngayon, kailan pa?"* ("If we do not act now, then when?")

### Emilio Jacinto — *Ang Utak ng Katipunan* — **real**
- **Who:** The "Brain of the Katipunan," and only about **20 years old** in 1896.
- **Wants:** To guide the Katipunan with its ideals; he wrote the **Kartilya** (its code of conduct) and edited its paper, *Kalayaan*.
- **Why he matters for kids:** A *young* person leading with intelligence. He's the one who teaches you the secret code.

### (Personal stake, woven into the story, not a card) — **fictional**
- The neighbours and friends you recruit are your own — the danger has faces you know.

## 4. Script

**FIL** (primary) then **EN** (translation). Beats kept short on purpose.

### P1 — A warning in the night · `story`

**FIL**
> Hatinggabi nang may kumatok.
> Hindi kaibigan. Hindi kalaban. Isang bulong.
> "Natuklasan na nila ang Katipunan."
> Tumigil ang iyong paghinga.

**EN**
> It was midnight when a knock came.
> Not a friend. Not an enemy. A whisper.
> "They have discovered the Katipunan."
> Your breath stopped.

### P2 — Who you are · `character` (Ikaw, fictional)

**FIL**
> Ikaw ang batang tagapaghatid ng Katipunan.
> Bata ka pa, kaya walang guwardiyang naghihinala sa iyo.
> Nakakalusot ka sa makikitid na eskinita ng Maynila.
> May dala kang mga salitang lihim — hindi kayang dalhin ninuman.

**EN**
> You are a young messenger of the Katipunan.
> You are only a child, so no guard suspects you.
> You slip through the narrow alleys of Manila.
> You carry secret words — words no one else can carry.

### P3 — What the Katipunan is · `story`

**FIL**
> Ano ang Katipunan?
> Isang lihim na kapatiran ng mga karaniwang Pilipino.
> Magsasaka, manggagawa, guro, bata — nagkaisa para sa iisang pangarap:
> kalayaan mula sa mga Espanyol.

**EN**
> What is the Katipunan?
> A secret brotherhood of ordinary Filipinos.
> Farmers, workers, teachers, children — united by one dream:
> freedom from Spanish rule.

### P4 — Andres Bonifacio · `character` (real)

**FIL**
> Si Andres Bonifacio ang Supremo — ang pinuno ng Katipunan.
> Hindi siya mayaman. Taga-Tondo, nagtatrabaho, at nag-aral mag-isa sa pagbabasa.
> Layunin niya: kalayaan para sa bawat Pilipino, kahit sa pinakamahirap.
> "Kung hindi tayo kikilos ngayon," tanong niya, "kailan pa?"

**EN**
> Andres Bonifacio was the Supremo — the leader of the Katipunan.
> He was not rich. A worker from Tondo who taught himself by reading.
> His goal: freedom for every Filipino, even the poorest.
> "If we do not act now," he asked, "then when?"

### P5 — Discovered · `story`

**FIL**
> Ngunit may nagsalita nang hindi dapat.
> Nalaman ng mga Espanyol ang tungkol sa Katipunan.
> Ngayon, hinahanap na nila ang mga kasapi. Delikado nang magtago.
> Kailangang magpasya — ngayon na.

**EN**
> But someone spoke who should not have.
> The Spanish learned about the Katipunan.
> Now they are hunting its members. Hiding is no longer safe.
> A choice had to be made — now.

## 5. Historical notes (verify before approval)

**Well documented — safe to keep**
- The Katipunan (KKK) was a secret revolutionary society founded by Andres Bonifacio and others in 1892, aiming for independence from Spain.
- It really used **secret codes, passwords, and coded writing**, and grew through careful, secret **recruitment** — so both mini-games are historically grounded.
- Bonifacio was from Tondo, of humble means, largely self-taught — the "ordinary people" framing is accurate and important.
- **Emilio Jacinto** was the young "Brain of the Katipunan," wrote the **Kartilya**, and edited *Kalayaan*. He was about 20 in 1896.
- In **August 1896** the Katipunan was exposed (via Teodoro Patiño), forcing the revolution to begin; Katipuneros **tore their cedulas** as the symbolic act of revolt. This is the widely taught (Agoncillo) narrative.

**⚠️ Contested — flag for your adviser (this is the big one)**
1. **"Pugad Lawin" vs "Balintawak," and the exact date.** Historians genuinely
   disagree about **where** and **when** the Cry happened. The traditional
   "Cry of Balintawak" (Aug 26, 1896) was later revised by historian Teodoro
   Agoncillo (following Pío Valenzuela) to the **"Cry of Pugad Lawin" (Aug 23,
   1896)** — and both the place and date remain disputed to this day.
   - This arc uses **"Pugad Lawin"** because that is the name in the DepEd/common
     curriculum, so it is defensible for a school tool. But your adviser should
     be aware of the controversy, and you may want a one-line teacher note.
2. **The precise details of the tearing/cry** (exact words, exact spot) come
   largely from later recollections and are debated. The script keeps it to the
   well-accepted essence and avoids over-claiming specifics.

**Invented (clearly fictional, per the fictional-ally design)**
- The player-messenger and their role.
- All specific dialogue (Bonifacio's and Jacinto's lines are dramatised).
