# Arc 3 — Paglaban ni Datu Bago (The Resistance of Datu Bago), Davao

**Status:** DRAFT for review. **NOT yet converted to game content.**
**Audience:** Grade 5 (about 10–11 years old)
**POV:** The pupil plays a *fictional ally* — a child of Datu Bago's riverside community.

> 🚨 **This arc needs the most careful validation of the three.** It is the
> **local Davao history your study exists to fill** — which means reliable,
> accessible sources are scarce, and it is easy to state something wrong with
> confidence. **Do not convert this to game content until your adviser / an
> Araling Panlipunan teacher / a Davao local-history source has checked §5.**
> Everything below is written conservatively and flags what must be verified.

---

## 1. Why this arc matters most (and is hardest)

Mactan and Pugad Lawin are national stories with mountains of sources. **Datu
Bago is the whole reason your capstone exists** — DepEd Region XI found *no
engaging local-history material* for Davao, and he is "absent from any existing
digital learning tool." So this arc is your study's unique contribution.

That's also the risk: with few sources, an AI (me included) can accidentally
invent detail. So this draft:
- uses **only** your manuscript's framing + the most widely-cited Davao facts,
- states **nothing** as a precise fact that isn't verifiable,
- and marks every claim that needs checking.

**An honest difference from the other two arcs:** Datu Bago **did not win.** The
Spanish took Davao. Handled well, that is a *more mature and truthful* lesson for
kids than "the hero always wins" — courage and unity matter even when you are
overcome, and remembering is itself a form of resistance. See beat D10–D11.

## 2. Design principles

| Principle | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Hook fast** | Open on unfamiliar ships entering the Davao Gulf — calm broken. |
| **The child is useful** | The ally knows the **river** — its bends, its mangroves — and warns/guides the community. (Kept distinct from Mactan's "reef" so it doesn't feel repeated: here it's about *warning and protecting people*, not repelling boats.) |
| **The teachable core** | Datu Bago **united different peoples** — Maguindanaon, Bagobo, and others — to defend a shared home. Unity of diverse groups is the heart. |
| **Local pride** | This is *Davao's own* hero. The tone should make a Davao child sit up: "this happened here." |
| **Honest outcome** | Bravery and unity, then defeat, then remembrance. Not sugar-coated, not grim. |
| **Respect** | Indigenous peoples portrayed with dignity and specificity, never generic "tribe." |

**Reading level:** short beats, 2–4 lines each.

## 3. Character roster

### Ikaw / You — *Batang taga-ilog (a child of the river community)* — **fictional**
- **Who:** An unnamed child of Datu Bago's community by the Davao River, ~11.
- **Wants:** To protect the people and place they love.
- **Superpower:** Knows the river — where to hide, where to pass, how to warn everyone in time.

### Datu Bago — *Punong datu ng Davao Gulf* — **real (verify details)**
- **Who (per your manuscript):** The Maguindanaon paramount datu of the Davao
  Gulf, who rallied local groups — including the Bagobo — against Spanish
  colonization.
- **Wants:** To keep Davao's peoples free and their home their own.
- **Why he matters for kids:** Davao had its *own* leader who stood against
  colonization — a name every Davaoeño child should know.
- **Note:** His personal details (exact origins, appearance, words) are **not**
  well documented. Give him dignity and resolve; invent no biography.

### José Oyanguren — *Pinuno ng ekspedisyong Espanyol* — **real (verify)**
- **Who (widely cited in Davao history — verify):** The Spaniard who led the
  1848 expedition that colonized the Davao Gulf and founded the town that became
  Davao, defeating Datu Bago's forces.
- **Framing:** Determined coloniser, not a cartoon villain — same "hard on
  colonisation, fair to the person" approach as Magellan.

### (Optional) A Bagobo elder/ally — **fictional representative**
- A dignified allied figure showing the union of peoples. **Would be an invented
  individual** (no named source), so tag as fictional — OR cut, and keep the
  alliance in narration only. *See open question Q2.*

## 5. HISTORICAL NOTES — verify before anything is finalised

**From your manuscript (already your study's stated framing)**
- Datu Bago = **Maguindanaon paramount datu** of the Davao Gulf who **rallied
  local indigenous groups, including the Bagobos**, against Spanish colonization.
- ⚠️ This means the current committed quiz item — *"Which group did Datu Bago
  **lead**?" → Bagobo* — is **slightly wrong**: he was Maguindanaon and *allied
  with* the Bagobo. **Fix on conversion** to "Which group did Datu Bago **ally
  with / rally**?" (answer: Bagobo), which matches the manuscript.

**Widely cited in Davao local history — CONFIRM with your adviser/sources**
1. **Year ~1848** and **José Oyanguren** (sometimes spelled Uyanguren) as the
   leader of the Spanish expedition that took the Davao Gulf and founded the town
   that became Davao. Commonly repeated, but **confirm the spelling, the exact
   year, and the details** before printing them as fact to children.
2. **Datu Bago's settlement along the Davao River**, and his **retreat upriver /
   relocation** after defeat. Plausible and commonly stated — **verify**.
3. The **outcome**: the resistance was **defeated** and the Spanish established
   the town. This is the accepted outcome — but confirm how your curriculum/
   adviser wants it phrased for pupils.

**Genuinely uncertain — do NOT state as fact**
- Datu Bago's birth, personal appearance, exact ethnicity certainty, and any
  **quotations**. All dialogue in this arc is dramatised (as in the other arcs).
- Precise troop numbers, exact battle locations, exact dates beyond the year.

**Cultural accuracy (see also the asset brief §8)**
- **Bagobo** material culture is specific and beautiful — abaca ikat textiles,
  intricate beadwork, brass ornaments and bells. If a Bagobo figure appears,
  reference **real** Bagobo material culture; never generic "tribal" costume.
- Maguindanaon and Bagobo are **different** peoples — don't blend them visually
  or textually.
- This whole arc's depiction should be reviewed by someone with Davao/Mindanao
  cultural knowledge, not only a general history teacher.

## 6. Draft script (PROVISIONAL — every fact pending §5 verification)

Written deliberately light on hard specifics. **FIL** then **EN**.

### D1 — Ships in the gulf · `story`
**FIL**
> Payapa ang umaga sa may Ilog Davao.
> Huni ng ibon. Amoy ng dagat at lupa.
> Pagkatapos — mga layag sa look. Hindi pamilyar.
> Isang bagay ang paparating.

**EN**
> The morning was calm by the Davao River.
> Birdsong. The smell of sea and earth.
> Then — sails in the gulf. Unfamiliar ones.
> Something was coming.

### D2 — Who you are · `character` (fictional)
**FIL**
> Ikaw ay batang lumaki sa tabi ng Ilog Davao.
> Kilala mo ang bawat liko, bawat bakawan.
> Alam mo kung saan magtatago at kung paano magbabala nang mabilis.
> Ito ang iyong tahanan.

**EN**
> You are a child raised beside the Davao River.
> You know every bend, every mangrove.
> You know where to hide and how to warn everyone quickly.
> This is your home.

### D4 — Datu Bago · `character` (real; keep general)
**FIL**
> Si Datu Bago ang punong datu ng lupain sa may Davao Gulf.
> Pinag-isa niya ang iba't ibang pangkat — Maguindanaon, Bagobo, at iba pa.
> Layunin niya: manatiling malaya ang kanilang tahanan.

**EN**
> Datu Bago was the paramount datu of the land around the Davao Gulf.
> He united different peoples — Maguindanaon, Bagobo, and others.
> His goal: to keep their home their own.

### D7 — The call to defend · `story` (teachable core)
**FIL**
> Hindi kayang labanan ng iisang pangkat ang mga mananakop.
> Kaya't nagkaisa sila — magkakaiba man ng wika at paniniwala.
> "Iisa ang ating tahanan," wika ni Datu Bago. "Iisa ang ating tindig."

**EN**
> No single group could face the colonisers alone.
> So they united — different in language and belief though they were.
> "Our home is one," said Datu Bago. "Our stand is one."

### D10 — The hard truth · `story`
**FIL**
> Buong tapang na lumaban ang mga taga-Davao.
> Ngunit napakalakas ng sandata ng mga mananakop.
> Natalo sila. Umatras si Datu Bago paakyat ng ilog.
> Naitatag ng mga Espanyol ang kanilang bayan.

**EN**
> The people of Davao fought with all their courage.
> But the colonisers' weapons were too strong.
> They were defeated. Datu Bago withdrew upriver.
> The Spanish established their town.

### D11 — Why we remember · `story`
**FIL**
> Ngunit hindi nakalimutan si Datu Bago.
> Siya ang unang nanindigan para sa Davao laban sa pananakop.
> Dahil sa tapang niya, may pangalan ang paglaban ng Davao.
> At ikaw, batang taga-ilog — bahagi ka ng kuwentong iyon.

**EN**
> But Datu Bago was not forgotten.
> He was the first to stand for Davao against colonisation.
> Because of his courage, Davao's resistance has a name.
> And you, child of the river — you are part of that story.

written once §5 is verified.)*
